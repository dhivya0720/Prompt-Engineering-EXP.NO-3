**STRAIGHTFORWARD PROMPTING**

**Definition**
Straightforward prompts are direct, clear, and concise instructions that expect a specific, factual response. They do not require elaborate context or prior questions — the prompt alone contains everything the AI needs to produce a useful output. These prompts are typically short and use imperative verbs such as Define, List, Explain, Name, or Describe. The expected output is usually a single sentence, short list, or a brief factual answer.

**Use Case 1 — Healthcare / Medical Information**

**Prompt 1:** List five common symptoms of Type 2 Diabetes that a patient should watch for.

**Output:**
Five common symptoms of Type 2 Diabetes: 1. Frequent urination (polyuria) — the kidneys work harder to filter excess glucose. 2. Excessive thirst (polydipsia) — fluid loss from urination leads to dehydration. 3. Unexplained weight loss — the body burns fat and muscle for energy when cells cannot absorb glucose. 4. Fatigue and low energy — cells are starved of glucose despite high blood sugar levels. 5. Blurred vision — high blood sugar causes the lens of the eye to swell, affecting focus

**Prompt 2:** Define insulin resistance in one sentence.

**Output:**
Insulin resistance is a condition in which the body's cells fail to respond effectively to the hormone insulin, causing the pancreas to produce more insulin to maintain normal blood glucose levels.

**3.PRECEDING QUESTION PROMPTING**

**Definition:**
Preceding Question prompting uses a two-part structure: first, a question is posed to establish context, and then a follow-up instruction asks the AI to elaborate or explain further. This technique guides the model to produce a more focused and contextually rich response.
The first part frames the topic and signals what is important; the second part directs the depth and direction of the answer. This style is highly effective for educational content, technical explanations, and policy discussions.

**Use Case 1 — Cybersecurity & Data Privacy**

**Prompt 1**: Why is data encryption critical in modern cybersecurity? Explain how symmetric and asymmetric encryption differ and when each should be used.

**Output:**
Data encryption is the backbone of modern cybersecurity — it ensures that even if data is intercepted, it remains unreadable to unauthorized parties.  Symmetric Encryption uses the same key for both encryption and decryption. It is faster and more efficient, making it ideal for encrypting large volumes of data (e.g., encrypting files or database records). Example: AES (Advanced Encryption Standard).  Asymmetric Encryption uses a pair of keys — a public key to encrypt and a private key to decrypt. It is slower but more secure for key exchange and authentication. Example: RSA, used in HTTPS and email signing.  In practice, many systems combine both: asymmetric encryption securely exchanges a symmetric session key, which is then used for the actual data transfer (as seen in TLS/SSL protocols).

**Prompt 2:** What is a phishing attack? Describe three warning signs that indicate an email may be a phishing attempt and how users can protect themselves.

**Output:**
A phishing attack is a cyberattack in which malicious actors impersonate trusted entities — such as banks, government agencies, or popular websites — to trick users into revealing sensitive information like passwords, credit card numbers, or login credentials.  Three warning signs of a phishing email: 1. Suspicious sender address — The email appears to come from a legitimate company but the actual email domain is slightly altered (e.g., support@paypa1.com instead of paypal.com). 2. Urgent or threatening language — Phrases like 'Your account will be suspended in 24 hours' create panic and pressure users to act without thinking. 3. Unexpected links or attachments — Hovering over links reveals URLs that don't match the displayed text; attachments may contain malware.  Users can protect themselves by: verifying the sender's email domain, never clicking suspicious links directly, enabling multi-factor authentication (MFA), and using anti-phishing browser extensions.



**Use Case 2 — Renewable Energy & Sustainability**

**Prompt 1**: Why is solar energy considered one of the most promising renewable energy sources? Explain how photovoltaic cells convert sunlight into electricity.
Output:
Solar energy is considered one of the most promising renewables because the sun provides an essentially unlimited and freely available source of energy. Unlike fossil fuels, solar power produces zero direct emissions during operation, and costs have fallen dramatically over the past decade, making it increasingly competitive.  How photovoltaic (PV) cells work: 1. Photons from sunlight strike the silicon semiconductor material in the solar cell. 2. The photon energy knocks electrons loose from their atoms, creating free electrons. 3. The internal electric field of the p-n junction directs these electrons in one direction, creating a direct current (DC). 4. An inverter then converts DC electricity into alternating current (AC), which can power homes and be fed into the electrical grid.

**Prompt 2:** What challenges do countries face when transitioning from fossil fuels to renewable energy? Discuss the role of energy storage technology in overcoming these challenges.

**Output:**
Transitioning from fossil fuels to renewables is one of the most complex challenges in modern energy policy. Key challenges include:  1. Intermittency — Solar and wind energy depend on weather conditions; they don't generate power at night or on calm days. 2. Grid infrastructure — Existing power grids were designed for centralized fossil fuel plants and need significant upgrades to handle distributed renewable sources. 3. Economic disruption — Communities dependent on coal and oil industries face job losses, requiring retraining and alternative economic development.  Role of energy storage technology: Advanced battery storage (e.g., lithium-ion, solid-state batteries) addresses the intermittency problem by storing excess energy generated during peak production and releasing it when demand exceeds supply. Grid-scale storage systems like Tesla's Megapack, pumped hydro storage, and emerging technologies like hydrogen fuel cells are crucial for making renewable grids stable and reliable 24/7.

**4. MISSING WORD PROMPTING**

**Definition:**
Missing Word prompting presents a statement with one or more blank spaces (represented as ____ or [blank]) that the AI is expected to fill in with the correct word, term, or phrase. This technique mirrors a fill-in-the-blank exercise format and is especially useful for knowledge testing, vocabulary reinforcement, and concept checking.
The structure naturally focuses the model on completing a specific factual gap rather than generating free-form text. It is widely used in educational AI tools, quiz generation, and language learning applications.

**Use Case 1 — Computer Networks & Internet Protocols**

**Prompt 1:** Fill in the blank: The protocol responsible for assigning unique IP addresses to devices on a network automatically is called ____.

**Output:**
The protocol responsible for assigning unique IP addresses to devices on a network automatically is called DHCP (Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol).  Explanation: DHCP operates on a client-server model. When a device connects to a network, it sends a DHCP Discover message; the DHCP server responds with an IP address offer, which the device accepts and the server confirms — a process known as the DORA handshake (Discover, Offer, Request, Acknowledge).

Prompt 2: Complete the sentence: In the OSI model, the layer responsible for end-to-end communication, error recovery, and flow control is the____ layer

**Output:**
In the OSI model, the layer responsible for end-to-end communication, error recovery, and flow control is the Transport layer.  Explanation: The Transport Layer (Layer 4) manages data segmentation and reassembly. It uses protocols such as TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) for reliable, connection-oriented communication and UDP (User Datagram Protocol) for faster, connectionless transmission. TCP ensures reliability through acknowledgments and retransmission of lost packets


**Use Case 2 — Artificial Intelligence Fundamentals**

**Prompt 1:** Fill in the blank: In a neural network, the mathematical function applied to a neuron's output to introduce non-linearity is called an ____ function.

**Output:**
In a neural network, the mathematical function applied to a neuron's output to introduce non-linearity is called an Activation function.  Explanation: Without activation functions, a neural network — no matter how many layers it has — would behave like a single linear transformation. Common activation functions include ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit), Sigmoid, Tanh, and Softmax. Each is chosen based on the layer's role and the nature of the problem (classification, regression, etc.).

**Prompt 2:** Complete the sentence: The process by which a machine learning model adjusts its internal parameters to minimize prediction error using gradient descent is called ____.

**Output:**
The process by which a machine learning model adjusts its internal parameters to minimize prediction error using gradient descent is called Backpropagation (or 'training').  Explanation: During backpropagation, the model calculates the gradient of the loss function with respect to each parameter by applying the chain rule of calculus. These gradients are then used by an optimizer (e.g., SGD, Adam) to update weights in the direction that reduces the loss, iteratively improving the model's accuracy over multiple training epochs.
